# Fig Tree (*Ficus carica*)

**Tier:** Existing baby | **Category:** Deciduous tree | **Survival estimate:** 70% | **Quantity:** 1

## Placement

The sunniest, hottest spot on the balcony — near the Pinus mugo zone. Figs need maximum heat to ripen and harden the new wood before winter. Unripened soft wood is killed by the first frost; fully ripened wood is significantly more cold-tolerant. Sun and heat are not problems for this plant — they are requirements.

## Watering

Heavy drinker — water daily in summer. During peak heat waves (38–42°C), twice daily watering may be necessary. Drought stress causes immediate and dramatic leaf drop — the fig does not give gradual warnings. In winter (after leaf drop): nearly dry, but not bone dry. Water only every 2–3 weeks in winter, and only on frost-free days.

## Feeding

High-potassium feed (tomato fertilizer) every 2 weeks May–August. Potassium drives fruit development and wood ripening. Avoid high-nitrogen feed, which promotes leafy growth at the expense of fruit and wood hardening.

## Winter Care

**CRITICAL for baby fig — follow this sequence:**

1. After leaf drop in November, wrap the entire plant (stems and canopy) in 2–3 layers of horticultural fleece
2. Wrap the pot in bubble wrap
3. Push against the building wall
4. If temperatures are forecast below -10°C for more than 2 consecutive days, move to an unheated stairwell, cool garage, or similar (below 10°C but frost-free is ideal — dormancy requires cool, not warm)
5. After year 3, once the wood has hardened through multiple seasons, the fig will tolerate -12 to -15°C without wrapping

## Additional Notes

**Variety matters significantly for cold hardiness.** 'Brown Turkey' and 'Brunswick' are the hardiest commonly available varieties, tolerating -12 to -15°C in the ground once mature. If the variety is unknown, assume it is tender and protect accordingly.

Root restriction in a container is actually beneficial for fig production — constrained roots trigger earlier and heavier fruiting. The fabric inner pot provides ideal root restriction plus the air-pruning that prevents root circling in a long-term container.

Prune to keep the plant under 1.5 m tall. A compact fig: survives wind far better, is far easier to wrap for winter, and is more practical on a balcony. Figs respond well to hard pruning — cut back to 3–4 main framework branches each February/March. Fruit forms on new growth from that season.

**Current size note:** If the plant is currently in a pot smaller than 20L, plan to move into this 40L fabric inner within 1–2 years. The larger volume is the target configuration, not necessarily the immediate setup.
