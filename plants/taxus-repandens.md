# Spreading Yew (*Taxus × media* 'Repandens')

**Tier:** 1 | **Category:** Evergreen Structure | **Survival estimate:** 95%

## Placement
Shaded inner Z-corner — the primary shade-zone structural plant. Does not need or want direct S-W sun. The Z-shape of the terrace creates a naturally sheltered, lower-light pocket; this is the ideal spot. Provides dark green evergreen structure where other plants struggle with shade.

## Pot Setup

| | Type | Volume | Dimensions (approx.) |
|---|---|---|---|

| **Outer pot** | Rigid, light-colored, no drain holes | 25L | ~Ø35cm x H30cm |

**System:** Double-wall with reservoir

### Pot layers (bottom to top)
1. **Drainage** — LECA: 2.3L
2. **Transition** — pure perlite: 0.8L
3. **Soil mix**: 10L
4. **Mulch** — 3cm bark (suits the shadier, moister corner environment)

## Watering
- **Summer (Jun–Aug):** Every 2–3 days. Shade reduces evaporation vs. sun zones but the corner can still get warm. Keep reservoir consistently topped.
- **Spring / Autumn:** Every 5–7 days.
- **Winter:** Weekly when above freezing. Yews dislike full drought even in winter.

## Feeding
Slow-release granular in March only. Light application — yews are slow-growing and do not need heavy feeding.

## Winter Care
Very cold hardy (to -25°C or below depending on variety). Plant is fine unprotected. Wrap outer pot to prevent freeze-thaw cracking. Maintain minimal watering through winter — never let roots completely dry.

## Additional Notes
Elegant low-spreading form. Grows slowly and maintains shape well without pruning. IMPORTANT: All parts of yew are highly toxic to humans, dogs, and most animals — berries especially. Be aware if children or pets have access to the terrace. Good structural evergreen for the shade corner where few other plants can provide reliable year-round greenery.
