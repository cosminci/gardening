# Boston Ivy (*Parthenocissus tricuspidata*)

**Tier:** 1 | **Category:** Climber | **Survival estimate:** 93%

## Placement
Sunny side wall — the primary wall-coverage climber for the S-W exposed faces. Self-clinging adhesive pads attach directly to masonry or concrete without needing a trellis, though initial guidance helps. Handles full sun, 38–42°C summer heat, and cold to -25°C. Spectacular scarlet-crimson autumn color before leaf drop.

## Watering
- **Summer (Jun–Aug):** Every 2 days. Vigorous climber with high water demand in heat. Monitor reservoir closely — can empty fast in 38–42°C.
- **Spring / Autumn:** Every 4–5 days.
- **Winter:** Weekly when above freezing. Deciduous but roots still need minimal moisture.

## Feeding
- **March:** Slow-release granular fertilizer.
- **May–August:** Liquid fertilizer (balanced or slightly higher N) every 2 weeks to support vigorous growth and maximize wall coverage speed.

## Winter Care
Hardy to -25°C. Fully deciduous — looks dead in winter, which is normal. Wrap outer pot. No other protection needed. Bare stems are actually attractive in winter light.

## Additional Notes
Superior to English ivy for S-W sunny wall positions — handles intense heat and sun far better. Self-clinging on most surfaces — guide the first-year shoots toward the wall with tape or clips until adhesive pads form. Grows fast once established (1–2m/year). Autumn colour is one of the best of any climbing plant — deep scarlet to crimson before leaf drop. Deciduous, so provides no winter screening.
