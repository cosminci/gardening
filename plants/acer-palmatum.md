# Japanese Maple (*Acer palmatum*)

**Tier:** Existing baby | **Category:** Deciduous tree | **Survival estimate:** 55% | **Quantity:** 1

## Placement

Shaded inner corner only — no exceptions. No direct afternoon sun, ever. Morning-only sun (before 10:00) or full dappled shade is acceptable. The S-W orientation means afternoon sun is intense and prolonged; even a brief period of direct sun on the delicate leaves causes irreversible scorch. Position BEHIND larger plants such as Pinus mugo or junipers so they act as a windbreak. This is the most demanding placement requirement on the balcony.

## Pot Setup

| | Type | Volume | Dimensions (approx.) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Inner pot** | Fabric (air pruning critical for delicate fibrous roots; prevents root circling) | 30L | ~Ø38 × H32 cm |
| **Outer pot** | Rigid, light-colored, no drain holes | 50L | ~Ø46 × H38 cm |

**System:** Double-wall with reservoir. Fabric pot bottom must NOT touch reservoir water — raise on 3–4 cm spacers to maintain an air gap beneath the fabric.

| Component | Ratio | Volume |

### Pot layers (bottom to top)
1. **Drainage** — LECA: 4.5L
2. **Transition** — pure perlite: 1.5L
3. **Soil mix**: 19.5L
4. **Mulch** — 6–7 cm bark mulch (root temperature insulation is critical)

## Watering

The trickiest plant on the balcony. Soil must be consistently moist but never waterlogged — Japanese maples rot quickly if roots sit in water. In summer heat waves, wind dries the pot rapidly while the shaded position creates a false sense of security. Check soil moisture daily by touch, 5 cm deep. Every 2 days in spring and autumn. Weekly in winter. Never allow the fabric pot to wick directly from standing reservoir water.

## Feeding

Half-strength ericaceous liquid fertilizer, monthly, April–July only. Do not feed after July. Do not use full-strength feed — burns leaf tips. Do not use non-ericaceous fertilizer — pH incompatibility. Less is more; over-feeding is a common cause of tip burn.

## Winter Care

Hardy to -20°C in ground. In a pot, effective root exposure approaches -10°C on Bucharest's coldest nights. Wrap pot in bubble wrap plus an outer layer of fleece. Group tightly with other pots against the building wall. If temperatures are forecast to drop below -12°C for more than 2 days, consider bringing to a bright, cold room (unheated stairwell, cool garage with light). Do not bring into a heated room.

## Additional Notes

**NEVER use standard Kekkilä universal mix** — pH too high, causes chlorosis. Group A acidic mix is mandatory.

**THE PRIMARY KILLER IS WIND.** The thin, deeply-lobed leaves desiccate rapidly in constant wind. On a 12th-floor S-W balcony, even a sheltered corner will experience significantly more air movement than ground level. Signs of wind stress: brown crispy margins on otherwise healthy leaves in summer, even with adequate watering. If this occurs, the position is too exposed and the plant may not survive long-term.

Mitigation strategies:
- Position directly behind Pinus mugo or junipers
- Mist foliage in the evening during heat waves (never in full sun)
- A temporary wind-break screen on the windward side in the first summer can be decisive

If it survives the first summer without serious leaf scorch, the prognosis improves substantially. If the first summer produces significant brown margins despite adequate water, wind is the cause — not drought.

Do not prune until the second or third year. Natural form is the goal. If the plant dies back to the crown in winter, do not discard until June — it may resprout from the base.
