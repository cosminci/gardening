# Honeysuckle (*Lonicera* spp.)

**Tier:** Existing baby | **Category:** Climber | **Survival estimate:** 85% | **Quantity:** 1

## Placement

Partial shade to sun. The ideal position is in the transition zone between the sunny and shaded areas — foliage can climb upward into more light while roots remain in a cooler, more sheltered position. Classic "cool feet, warm head" configuration.

## Pot Setup

| | Type | Volume | Dimensions (approx.) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Inner pot** | Plastic (vigorous enough that fabric not needed) | 25L | ~Ø35 × H30 cm |
| **Outer pot** | Rigid, light-colored, no drain holes | 40L | ~Ø42 × H35 cm |

**System:** Double-wall with reservoir

| Component | Ratio | Volume |

### Pot layers (bottom to top)
1. **Drainage** — LECA: 3.8L
2. **Transition** — pure perlite: 1.3L
3. **Soil mix**: 16.3L
4. **Mulch** — 5–7 cm bark mulch (MANDATORY — see notes)

## Watering

Consistent moisture is essential — roots must never fully dry out, especially while the plant is still a baby and not yet established. Daily in summer. Every 2–3 days in spring and autumn. Weekly in winter.

## Feeding

Slow-release granular fertilizer in March. Liquid balanced fertilizer every 2 weeks May–August.

## Winter Care

Most Lonicera species are hardy to -20°C. Wrap pot in bubble wrap November–March. The top growth may die back partially in a hard winter — this is normal; new growth emerges vigorously from the base in spring. Do not cut back in autumn; wait until new growth confirms which stems are alive in spring.

## Additional Notes

**Trellis or wires are mandatory.** Tie young stems loosely with soft ties — wind will snap unsecured climbing growth. On a 12th-floor balcony, any unsecured stem becomes a whip in the wind. As the plant establishes, stems become more robust and need less attention, but all new growth should be tied in as it extends.

**MULCH HEAVILY — this is the #1 rule for Lonicera in containers.** "Cool feet, warm head" is not optional for this genus. Roots must be kept cool and moist while the foliage is in warmth and light. A 5–7 cm bark mulch layer over the pot surface is essential; without it, root overheating in the S-W summer exposure will severely stress or kill the plant.

**Starting size note:** If currently in a pot smaller than 10L, that is fine for the first season. Move to this 25L inner pot configuration within a year — Lonicera has vigorous roots that will quickly outgrow a small container, and root restriction causes premature stress in this species.

Fragrant flowers (typically white to yellow, depending on species/cultivar) attract pollinators — bees and hoverflies. Flowering period is typically June–September, often with a second flush after trimming. The fragrance is strongest in the evening.
