# Elephant's Ears (*Bergenia cordifolia*)

**Tier:** 1 | **Category:** Perennial | **Survival estimate:** 93% | **Quantity:** 2

## Placement
Shade corner. Evergreen with large, leathery leaves that resist wind damage. Provides bold visual weight in low-light zones where most ornamentals struggle.

## Pot Setup

| | Type | Volume | Dimensions (approx.) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Inner pot** | Plastic | 10L | ~27cm Ø × 22cm H |
| **Outer pot** | Rigid, light-colored, no drain holes | 15L | ~30cm Ø × 25cm H |

**System:** Double-wall or shared tray

## Soil Mix — Group C: Moisture-Loving

| Component | Ratio | Volume |
|---|---|---|
| Kekkilä universal | 50% | 3.3L |
| Perlite | 20% | 1.3L |
| Composted bark | 15% | 1.0L |
| LECA (in mix) | 10% | 0.6L |
| Vermiculite | 5% | 0.3L |
| **Total soil mix** | | **6.5L** |

### Pot layers (bottom to top)
1. **Drainage** — LECA: 1.5L
2. **Transition** — pure perlite: 0.5L
3. **Soil mix**: 6.5L
4. **Mulch** — 2-3cm composted bark (retains moisture, suits shade conditions)

## Watering
- **Summer:** Every 2-3 days. Tolerates some drought but performs best with consistent moisture.
- **Winter:** Weekly. Evergreen — continues to transpire through winter and should not be allowed to dry out completely.

## Feeding
- Slow-release granular in March.
- Light liquid feed May–July to support foliage and flowering.

## Winter Care
- Hardy to -30°C. Wrap pot.
- Evergreen — leaves turn an attractive bronze/red in cold weather, adding winter interest.
- No cutting back needed; old leaves protect the crown and contribute to the display.

## Additional Notes
- Large leathery leaves (20-30cm across) provide strong visual weight in the shade corner.
- Pink flowers on upright stems in early spring (March–April) — one of the earliest blooms on the terrace.
- Very low maintenance once established. Tolerates deep shade, wind, drought, and cold.
- Pairs well with Taxus and ivy in the shade zone — contrasting textures work effectively together.
- ×2 plants fills the shade corner with presence without overcrowding.
