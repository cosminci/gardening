# Trumpet Vine (*Campsis radicans*)

**Tier:** 1 | **Category:** Climber | **Survival estimate:** 90%

## Placement
Sunny side wall — specifically the hottest, most sun-exposed position available. Campsis is a heat-lover that actually performs better with more sun and warmth. The 38–42°C summer temperatures that challenge other plants are ideal for this species. Produces spectacular large orange-red trumpet flowers July–September.

## Watering
- **Summer (Jun–Aug):** Every 2 days. Large plant with high transpiration in full sun and heat. Reservoir should be checked every 2–3 days minimum.
- **Spring / Autumn:** Every 4–5 days.
- **Winter:** Weekly when above freezing. Deciduous — minimal needs after leaf drop.

## Feeding
- **March:** Slow-release granular fertilizer (use one with good phosphorus content to support flowering).
- **May–August:** Liquid fertilizer every 2 weeks, using a formula with higher P and K (flower-promoting) rather than high N which promotes leaves over flowers.

## Winter Care
Hardy to -25°C. Deciduous — goes completely dormant. Wrap outer pot well (it is the largest pot in the climber group). The plant itself needs no additional protection at USDA Zone 6b. Prune back hard in late February before new growth begins — this stimulates the best flowering on new wood.

## Additional Notes
Needs a strong, well-anchored trellis or wire system — mature plants develop woody stems and significant weight. Initial years (1–2) may produce limited flowers; full flowering performance develops from year 3 onward. Prune aggressively each February: cut all stems back to 2–3 buds from the base framework. This looks dramatic but produces the best summer flower show. Can be somewhat invasive in garden soil — not a concern in a container. Deciduous, so provides no winter screening. The orange trumpet flowers are excellent for attracting pollinators.
