# Olive Tree (*Olea europaea*)

**Tier:** Existing baby | **Category:** Evergreen tree | **Survival estimate:** 65% | **Quantity:** 1

## Placement

Maximum sun — the hottest, most sun-exposed spot on the balcony, near the Pinus mugo zone. Olives evolved in the Mediterranean and thrive in intense heat and full sun. Do not shade this plant.

## Soil Mix — Group B (modified): Mediterranean — extra drainage

Standard Group B modified with additional perlite/gravel. Baby olives need maximum drainage above all else.

| Component | Ratio | Volume |
|---|---|---|
| Kekkilä universal | 30% | 5.85L |
| Perlite | 35% | 6.83L |
| Coarse sand / gravel | 25% | 4.88L |
| LECA | 10% | 1.95L |
| **Total soil mix** | | **19.5L** |

### Pot layers (bottom to top)
1. **Drainage** — LECA: 4.5L
2. **Transition** — pure perlite: 1.5L
3. **Soil mix**: 19.5L
4. **Mulch** — 3–4 cm coarse grit or fine gravel (not bark — retains too much moisture)

## Watering

Less than you think. Let the top 5 cm of soil dry out completely before watering — probe with a finger, not by visual inspection. Every 3–4 days in summer at most; in a heat wave, potentially every 2 days, but only after the surface has dried. Every 2–3 weeks in winter. Overwatering kills more baby olives than cold does. When in doubt, do not water.

## Feeding

Light. Slow-release balanced granular fertilizer in March only. No liquid feeding. Olives are adapted to nutrient-poor soils — overfeeding produces lush growth that is cold-tender.

## Winter Care

**CRITICAL — baby olive cold limits:**
- Baby olive trees die below -8 to -10°C. Adults tolerate brief exposure to -12°C.
- From November: wrap pot AND trunk/canopy in 2 layers of horticultural fleece.
- Keep pressed against the building wall on all cold nights.
- Keep soil almost completely dry through winter — wet soil + freezing temperatures = root death. Water only if there has been no frost for several days and soil is bone dry.
- If a prolonged cold snap below -10°C is forecast (several days), bring the plant indoors to a bright, cool room: 5–10°C is ideal. An unheated stairwell with a window, or a cool spare room. Do not bring into heated living space — the warm dry air is harmful.
- Return outdoors once temperatures stabilise above -5°C.

## Additional Notes

Do not expect olive fruit production in Bucharest — the winters are too cold for reliable cropping. This is an ornamental specimen. The silver-grey foliage is beautiful year-round and moves gracefully in wind. The gnarled trunk will develop character over years.

As the tree matures and hardens (after 3–5 years), cold tolerance improves significantly. The first two winters are the most critical. Year-one and year-two plants have not yet built the wood hardness that provides cold resistance. Survive those winters and the olive becomes substantially more viable.

If the canopy dies back in a cold winter but the root is alive, the olive may resprout from the base in spring. Do not discard until June if there is any doubt.
