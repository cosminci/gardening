# Soil Mixes Reference
## Bucharest 12th-Floor Balcony (S-W, 36sqm Z-terrace)

---

## Why Standard Potting Mix Fails Here

Cheap universal potting mix is the number-one silent killer on this terrace. The problem is not what it contains — it is what it does under extreme conditions.

Standard peat-based potting mix behaves well when kept evenly moist. On a 12th-floor S-W terrace in July, with 40°C temperatures and constant drying wind, the mix dries completely between waterings. Once peat-based soil dries below a critical threshold, it becomes **hydrophobic** — it repels water instead of absorbing it. Water poured on top runs down the gap between the soil and pot walls, straight out the drainage holes, without wetting the root zone at all. The plant dies of thirst while you think you are watering it.

Secondary failures of standard mix on this terrace:
- **Compaction**: After 2-3 months of watering and drying cycles, peat collapses into a dense mass. Roots suffocate. Drainage fails.
- **No insulation value**: Dense wet compost conducts heat directly to roots. A dark plastic pot in direct sun with standard mix can reach 55°C at root level.
- **pH drift**: Standard mixes are designed for moderate conditions. Intense heat and frequent watering accelerate nutrient leaching and pH changes.

All four soil groups below include perlite and bark specifically to prevent compaction and hydrophobicity. None of them should be purchased from discount garden centers — use quality branded components.

## Additives for ALL Mixes

These three additives go into every mix regardless of group:

**Mycorrhizal fungi inoculant** (critical): Mycorrhizae form a symbiotic network with plant roots, extending their effective reach 10 to 100 times. In a pot — where root expansion is limited — this is not optional. The fungi effectively give the plant access to a much larger volume of soil moisture and nutrients than the pot physically contains. Add at planting: sprinkle powder directly on roots before backfilling, or mix granules into soil at 5g per 10L. Brands: RootGrow, Mycorrhiza+ (Substral), Mykos. Do not apply with fungicides.

**Hydrogel crystals** (skip for Group B): Crystals absorb 200-400x their weight in water and release it slowly as soil dries. Add 5g per 10L of soil mix, pre-soaked for 30 minutes before mixing in. **Do not use in Group B (Mediterranean)** — permanently moist soil around lavender and olive roots causes rot.

**Wetting agent**: A few drops of diluted horticultural wetting agent (e.g., Grotek Wet Set, or plain diluted washing-up liquid at 1 drop per 5L water) added to the first watering after planting re-hydrates dry components and ensures even moisture distribution. Repeat every 3 months or if water starts beading on soil surface.

## Group A: Acid Mix (pH 5.5-6.5)

**For**: Japanese maple (Acer palmatum), Magnolia Susan

These plants evolved in forest environments with acidic, humus-rich, well-draining soil. They cannot tolerate alkaline conditions or waterlogging. They also cannot tolerate root heat — the acid mix combined with double-wall pot insulation is essential.

| Component | Proportion | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Kekkilä Rhododendron/Ericaceous mix | 60% | Acid base, humus-rich |
| Perlite | 20% | Drainage, aeration, prevents compaction |
| Bark chips (medium grade, 10-20mm) | 20% | Structure, slow decomposition, acid buffer |

**pH target**: 5.5-6.5. Test annually with a cheap pH meter. If pH creeps above 6.5, add a small amount of sulphur granules or switch to rainwater for irrigation (Bucharest tap water is slightly alkaline).

**Notes**: Never use standard potting mix as a substitute for ericaceous mix in Group A. The pH difference causes chlorosis (yellowing leaves) in maples and magnolias within weeks.

## Group B: Mediterranean Mix (pH 7-8)

**For**: Olive (Olea europaea), Lavender (Lavandula angustifolia), Sedum, Sempervivum, Festuca glauca, Stipa tenuissima

These plants evolved in poor, rocky, alkaline, fast-draining soils. They store water in leaves and stems. They are killed by moisture retention around roots. This mix is intentionally poor and gritty.

| Component | Proportion | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Kekkilä Universal potting mix | 40% | Base nutrition |
| Perlite | 30% | Maximum drainage |
| Coarse sand or fine gravel (2-5mm) | 20% | Weight, grit, drainage |
| LECA (clay pebbles, 4-8mm) | 10% | Air pockets, drainage |

**pH target**: 7.0-8.0. If using collected rainwater, add a small pinch of ground limestone per 10L to maintain alkalinity.

**No hydrogel**: Omit entirely. These plants need the soil to dry out completely between waterings.

**Notes for lavender**: Even this mix may not be gritty enough if the pot does not drain instantly. See lavender-post-mortem.md for the specific lavender mix (60% soil + 40% perlite/gravel, even higher drainage than Group B standard).

## Group C: Moisture-Loving Mix (pH 6.0-7.0)

**For**: Fig (Ficus carica), Clematis, Lonicera japonica, Hedera (ivy), Hydrangea, Rosa rugosa, Campsis radicans, Parthenocissus, Fallopia baldschuanica, Bergenia

These are vigorous growers or climbers with high water demand. They need a mix that retains moisture well but never becomes waterlogged. The balance between retention and drainage is achieved by combining medium-retention components with aeration materials.

| Component | Proportion | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Kekkilä Universal potting mix | 50% | Moisture retention, nutrition base |
| Perlite | 20% | Drainage, prevents compaction |
| Bark chips (fine to medium, 10-15mm) | 15% | Structure, slow decomposition |
| LECA (clay pebbles) | 10% | Air pockets at root level |
| Vermiculite | 5% | Moisture retention, micronutrients |

**pH target**: 6.0-7.0. Hydrangea color (for pink/blue varieties) can be influenced — more acid soil gives blue flowers, more alkaline gives pink. Adjust deliberately if desired.

**Notes for climbers**: Campsis, Parthenocissus, and Fallopia are extremely vigorous. Repot or root-prune every 2-3 years. Use the largest possible pot — 60-80L minimum for mature plants.

---

## Group D: Conifer and Evergreen Mix (pH 6.0-7.0)

**For**: Pinus mugo, Junipers (Juniperus squamata, J. horizontalis, J. communis), Taxus baccata, Buxus sempervirens, Euonymus fortunei, Cotoneaster horizontalis

Conifers and tough evergreens need consistent drainage and moderate fertility. They dislike wet roots but also dislike complete dryout — they continue slow transpiration through needles and leaves even in winter.

| Component | Proportion | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Kekkilä Universal potting mix | 60% | Base nutrition, structure |
| Perlite | 25% | Drainage, aeration |
| Bark chips (medium grade) | 15% | Structure, moisture buffer |

**pH target**: 6.0-7.0.

**Notes for Pinus mugo**: Use an 80L+ pot. The 25% perlite is important — Pinus mugo suffers immediately in waterlogged or compacted soil. Mycorrhizal inoculant is especially beneficial for pines.

## The Layer System

Every pot (regardless of soil group) is filled in layers from bottom to top. This creates drainage zones, prevents soil from migrating into the reservoir, and buffers the transition between soil types.

**Layer 1 — Bottom drainage (5cm)**: LECA clay pebbles, 8-16mm grade. This layer sits directly above the drainage holes. It prevents soil from washing out and creates a free-draining zone. In double-wall pots, this is above the inner pot drainage holes, above the air gap.

**Layer 2 — Fabric barrier**: A single layer of landscape fabric (geotextile) cut to pot diameter. Laid on top of LECA. This prevents soil mix from migrating down into the LECA layer over repeated waterings while allowing water to flow freely.

**Layer 3 — Perlite transition (3cm)**: Coarse perlite layer above the fabric. Additional insurance against compaction at the bottom of the root zone. Especially important for Group B plants.

**Layer 4 — Soil mix**: The appropriate group mix for the plant, filling the pot to within 5-6cm of the rim. Press gently — do not compact. Leave room for roots to settle naturally.

**Layer 5 — Mulch (5cm)**: Cover the soil surface completely. Use bark chips, coco coir, or gravel (gravel for Group B Mediterranean plants). Mulch achieves three things simultaneously: reduces surface evaporation by 30-50%, insulates soil surface from direct sun heat, and suppresses weed seeds. This single addition can reduce watering frequency significantly in summer. Refresh mulch every spring — it breaks down slowly over winter.

## Wicking Cord Setup in Soil

For double-wall System A pots, the cotton wick must be embedded correctly for capillary action to work:

1. Before filling the pot, thread the wick through a drainage hole, leaving 15-20cm hanging below the pot (this will extend into the reservoir).
2. Fill with the LECA and fabric layers.
3. Coil the internal end of the wick in a loose circle on top of the fabric layer, then add the perlite transition layer on top.
4. Fill with soil mix, ensuring the coiled portion of the wick is buried 5-10cm deep in the soil mix.
5. The coiling creates a larger contact area with the soil, improving capillary draw.
6. For pots over 40cm diameter, use two wicks, placed on opposite sides.

The wick only works when it is continuously damp — dry cotton loses capillary function. When first setting up a new pot, water the soil from the top until water drips from the drainage holes, ensuring the wick is wet from soil to reservoir before switching to reservoir-only watering.
