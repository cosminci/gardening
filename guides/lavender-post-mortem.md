# Lavender Post-Mortem
## Why It Died and How to Succeed Next Time
## Bucharest 12th-Floor Balcony (S-W, 36sqm Z-terrace)

---

## The Frustrating Irony

Lavender looks like the perfect balcony plant for a hot S-W terrace. It is Mediterranean. It loves sun. It is drought-tolerant. It is fragrant. And yet it keeps dying on Bucharest balconies, including this one.

The reasons are specific and fixable. Lavender does not die here because the conditions are wrong — it dies because of avoidable mistakes that can be corrected with the right species, the right soil, and the right watering discipline.

---

## Most Likely Killers (in Order of Probability)

### Killer 1: Overwatering Combined with Wrong Soil

This is the most common cause of lavender death, and it kills the plant so gradually that the cause is not obvious until the plant is already dead.

Lavender evolved in rocky, alkaline, nutrient-poor soils on Mediterranean hillsides. These soils drain instantly and completely. A lavender root sitting in moisture for more than a few hours develops rot. The rot progresses up from the root zone before any symptoms are visible above ground. By the time the plant wilts or turns grey-brown, the root system is already gone.

Standard potting mix retains moisture. On a 12th-floor terrace, this seems like an advantage — you water less often. For lavender, it is a slow death sentence. The mix holds water around the roots for days, exactly long enough for rot to establish between each watering.

The problem compounds: a plant with developing root rot cannot uptake water efficiently, so leaves look wilted even when the soil is damp. The instinct is to water more. More water accelerates the rot. The plant dies from what looks like drought but is actually drowning.

**The fix**: The soil mix must drain so fast it seems wrong. See the Correct Mix section below.

### Killer 2: Winter Wet + Cold Combination

Lavender can tolerate -10°C to -15°C when it goes into winter in the right condition: dry, hardened growth, fast-draining soil.

It cannot tolerate -5°C when the soil is damp. The mechanism is the same as with Mediterranean plants generally (see winter-protection.md) — wet soil freezes in a way that ruptures root cells. The plant may look fine going into January and be dead by March.

The Bucharest pattern is particularly dangerous: mild wet Novembers followed by sudden hard frosts in December or January. A lavender that was being watered through a warm November has damp soil when the hard frost arrives.

**The fix**: Stop all watering in October. Keep lavender almost completely dry from October through March. If it rains heavily, check that drainage is working and the pot is not holding water.

### Killer 3: Wrong Species — French Lavender (Lavandula stoechas)

Lavandula stoechas — French or Spanish lavender, the one with butterfly-wing bracts on top of the flower head — is widely sold at Romanian garden centers in spring. It looks beautiful in the nursery. It is also rated to around USDA zone 7b to 8, meaning it is not reliably hardy below -5°C to -7°C.

Bucharest winters routinely go below -10°C, sometimes to -18°C or -20°C. Lavandula stoechas planted in spring is dead before Christmas in any average winter.

This is not bad luck. It is a species-selection error.

**The fix**: Never buy French lavender for this terrace. See the Correct Species section below.

### Additional Contributing Factors

- **Pot with saucer**: Lavender in a saucer after rain is lavender with wet feet for 12-24 hours. That is enough to begin root rot.
- **Too-large pot**: A large pot holds more moisture per plant root-volume ratio. Lavender roots a 15-20cm plant in a 30-40L pot will sit in persistently damp soil in the outer pot volume. Size the pot close to the root ball — a snug fit.
- **Autumn pruning**: Cutting into lavender in autumn removes the woody material that protects the growing points over winter. Cut lavender in autumn often does not survive. Prune only in spring.
- **Cutting into old wood**: Lavender does not regenerate from old brown woody stems. If you cut back past the green growth zone into woody material, that branch is dead. This is why old overgrown lavender cannot be hard-pruned back to health — it needs replacement. When in doubt, take out less rather than more.

## The Correct Species

**Buy Lavandula angustifolia specifically.** This is true English lavender, rated USDA zone 5-6, reliably hardy to -20°C when in the right soil conditions.

### Best Varieties for This Terrace

| Variety | Height | Cold Hardiness | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 'Hidcote' | 40-50cm | USDA 5 | Deep purple, compact, very reliable |
| 'Munstead' | 40-45cm | USDA 5 | Slightly shorter, early flowering |
| 'Imperial Gem' | 45-55cm | USDA 5 | Good compact habit |
| 'Vera' | 60-70cm | USDA 5 | Taller, more fragrant |

**What to avoid**:
- Any labeled only as "lavender" or "lavandula" without the species name — likely stoechas
- Lavandula stoechas (any variety with butterfly bracts)
- Lavandula x intermedia (Lavandin) — hardier than stoechas but less so than angustifolia, and the hardiness is inconsistent
- Any lavender sold in a 9cm nursery pot in April with flowers already open — these are usually forced and may be stoechas

**Where to buy in Romania**: Hornbach and Leroy Merlin occasionally stock correctly labeled Lavandula angustifolia. Specialist nurseries are more reliable for correct species identification. Online from Polish or German nurseries (they ship to Romania and their labeling tends to be accurate for cold-hardy varieties).

## The Correct Soil Mix

This is the non-negotiable fix. No other change matters if the soil retains moisture.

**Lavender-specific mix**:
- 60% potting mix (Kekkilä universal or similar quality)
- 40% perlite + coarse sand/gravel combined

The mix should feel gritty and visibly chunky. Water poured on top should drain through and exit the pot within 60 seconds. If water pools on top for more than a minute, the mix is too water-retentive.

This is even grittier than the standard Group B Mediterranean mix (which is 30% perlite + 20% sand). Lavender is the most drainage-demanding plant on this terrace.

**No hydrogel crystals.** Ever. Under any circumstances.

**No water-retaining additives of any kind.**

**Pot size**: Snug, not large. A 20-25L pot for a mature lavender plant. The roots should fill most of the pot volume.

**Pot material**: Terracotta preferred. Terracotta breathes and dries faster than plastic. This is usually a disadvantage on this terrace — for lavender it is exactly right.

---

## Correct Watering

**In plain language**: water only when the soil is completely, bone dry.

Push your finger to the full depth of the pot. If there is any moisture anywhere, do not water. Wait another day or two. Water when the soil is dry throughout.

**Summer**: Every 5-10 days depending on heat. Lavender in direct S-W sun in August will dry faster, but still less often than you think.

**Spring and autumn**: Every 10-14 days at most. Often less.

**Winter**: Once in November to settle the plant. Then nothing, or once in January-February if there has been absolutely no precipitation for 6+ weeks and the plant looks distressed.

**Never**: Let water sit in a saucer. Use no saucer at all under lavender pots. The pot sits on feet on bare terrace tiles, not in any tray.

**Never**: Water after the first of October.

---

## Correct Sun

Full sun. The most exposed, most sun-saturated position on the terrace. Zone 1 or Zone 2 as described in layout.md. A minimum of 6 hours direct sun per day — more is better. Lavender in part shade does not die quickly but it grows weak and floppy, and the flower production is poor.

The full S-W sun exposure that kills so many other plants is exactly what lavender wants.

---

## Correct Winter Care

1. Do not prune in autumn.
2. Stop watering by early October.
3. Ensure pot drainage is completely clear.
4. Wrap the pot (not the plant) with a single layer of bubble wrap or coir mat for insulation.
5. Do not move lavender indoors — it needs cold dormancy. It just needs cold-dry, not cold-wet.
6. Do not cover the foliage — air circulation around the plant in winter prevents fungal issues.
7. Remove pot wrap in March.
8. Prune lightly in March-April: cut back to green growth, shaping to a neat mound. Never cut into brown woody material.

---

## Summary: The Lavender Success Checklist

- Species: Lavandula angustifolia only ('Hidcote' or 'Munstead' recommended)
- Soil: 60% potting mix + 40% perlite/gravel, no hydrogel, no water-retaining additives
- Pot: Terracotta, snug fit, no saucer, excellent drainage
- Sun: Full sun, Zone 1 or Zone 2
- Watering: Only when completely dry throughout the pot. Never in winter
- Winter: Keep dry. Wrap pot, not plant. Do not prune in autumn
- Spring pruning: Light trim to green growth only, never into old wood
